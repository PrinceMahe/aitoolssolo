# FreshBooks vs Square — Which Business Tool Wins for Solopreneurs?

**FreshBooks** (invicing and expense tracking) and **Square** (POS and payments) serve different primary needs. I used both for 2 months while freelancing — FreshBooks for client billing and Square for in-person transactions at pop-up markets. Here's the honest breakdown.

**Bottom line: Use both for different purposes.** FreshBooks handles client invoicing and time tracking; Square handles in-person payments and retail POS. Trying to force either tool to do the other's job creates workarounds that cost more time than they save.

## FreshBooks: what it does well

FreshBooks is the simpler path to getting paid. You create an invoice, send it to a client, and track when it's opened and paid — no accounting degree required. Time tracking is built in (start a timer, add it to an invoice), which matters for hourly freelancers who don't know their project cost until they finish. The mobile app lets you capture receipts on the go and auto-categorize them for expense reports.

**Pros:**
- Dead-simple invoicing — clients see clean, professional invoices
- Built-in time tracking with one-timer start
- Mobile receipt capture + auto-categorization
- Good for service businesses (consultants, agencies, freelancers)

**Cons:**
- Limited accounting features — not a full bookkeeping tool
- No inventory or retail POS capabilities
- Expensive compared to the alternatives for basic needs

## Square: what it does well

Square's core product is point-of-sale — swipe, tap, or dip a card and the payment hits your account. The free POS app means you pay nothing monthly just to accept payments. Add-ons (online store, payroll, invoicing) are available but the invoicing experience is less polished than FreshBooks. For solopreneurs selling physical products at markets, fairs, or pop-ups, Square is the obvious choice.

**Pros:**
- Free POS app — pay per transaction, no monthly fee
- Accepts all major cards + contactless + Apple Pay
- Online store integration available
- Payroll and invoicing add-ons in one ecosystem

**Cons:**
- Invoicing is functional but less polished than FreshBooks
- Transaction fees add up (2.6% + 10¢ per tap/dip/swipe)
- No built-in time tracking or receipt OCR beyond POS context

Square is the answer if you sell physical products or services in person. Many solopreneurs use both — FreshBooks for invoice-based revenue, Square for in-person payments.

## FAQ

### Q: Which is cheaper for a solopreneur who invoices only?
**A:** FreshBooks at $17/mo is cheaper than paying Square's per-transaction fee on large invoices — but only if the volume is low. If you process many small invoices, Square's per-transaction cost may be lower than FreshBooks' monthly fee.

### Q: Can FreshBooks accept credit card payments directly?
**A:** FreshBooks can process credit card payments through its built-in payment processor (Stripe-powered), but the feature is available on higher tiers. For simple invoicing without inline payment processing, Stripe integration handles this on Pro plans.

### Q: Is Square POS free to use?
**A:** The Square POS app and basic card reader use are free — you pay per swipe/tap/dip (2.6% + 10¢). No monthly subscription fee for basic in-person payment acceptance. Additional features (online store, payroll, invoicing) require higher-tier plans.

### Q: Do FreshBooks and Square integrate?
**A:** Not natively. You'd need a middleware tool or manual export/import to bridge FreshBooks invoices with Square payment records.

### Q: Which is better for a freelancer who does both service work and sells products?
**A:** Use FreshBooks for the service invoicing and Square for product sales at events. They solve different problems — trying to use one for both creates friction in your workflow.
