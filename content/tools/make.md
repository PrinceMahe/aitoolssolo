# Make — Full Review & Verdict

Make is the visual no-code automation platform that lets you chain together apps with branching scenarios, and for an ops person or agency owner who's outgrown simple "if-this-then-that" tools, it's one of the most capable options on the market. It's more powerful than Zapier for complex logic and more visual than most rivals, but that power comes with a real learning curve.

**Verdict: 4.4/5.** Best for operations managers and agencies who need branching, multi-step automations that don't fit into a one-trigger-one-action flow. The free tier is useful for testing; the paid plans are fair for anyone automating daily.

## What it is

Make (formerly Integromat, rebranded in 2020) is a visual automation platform owned by Celonis. Where Zapier models everything as trigger + action, Make gives you a canvas where you **visually draw the flow** — modules, routers, branching conditions, loops, and error handlers — and see exactly how data moves between them. It's aimed at people who don't want to write code but need automations complex enough that a linear A→B workflow won't cut it.

## How it works / key features

**Visual scenario builder.** The core of Make. You drag modules onto a canvas and connect them with links, wiring each step's output into the next module's input. It's opaque code made visible — you can see a whole multi-branch flow at a glance instead of reading a long chain of triggers.

**Branching and routers.** This is where Make beats simpler tools. A router lets you fork a scenario into different paths based on conditions, so the same flow can behave differently depending on the data that comes in — e.g. route low-value leads to one sequence and high-value leads to another.

**1,500+ app integrations.** Make connects to a huge catalog of apps and services, including webhooks for anything without a native module. The connection management is solid, and you can map fields between apps directly on the canvas.

**Data stores and webhooks.** Beyond pure app-chaining, Make gives you built-in data storage for persisting state between scenario runs, plus live webhooks so external systems can trigger scenarios. For solo builders this turns Make into a lightweight backend.

**Error handling and monitoring.** Each scenario accepts error handlers, partial-execution retries, and you get a clean run history to see exactly where a step failed. That observability is what makes it usable for real production automations rather than toy scripts.

## Where it shines / where it falls short

**Strengths**
- **Branching and complex logic** that simpler automation tools can't express — this is the headline reason to choose Make.
- **Fully visual editor** — you can understand an automation at a glance and debug it by looking at it.
- **Huge integration catalog plus webhooks**, so almost anything can be connected.
- **Good error handling** and run history for production-grade reliability.

**Weaknesses**
- **Steep learning curve** — the canvas, routers, and data model are far more intimidating than a point-and-click workflow.
- **Operations limits** on lower plans can be hit quickly by chatty integrations.
- **Not for simple one-off tasks** — for a single A→B sync, a simpler tool is faster to set up.

## Best for / not for

Best for **operations teams** and **agencies** that need to orchestrate data across many systems with real branching and conditions, and for solo builders who are comfortable with a visual-but-technical model.

Not ideal for absolute beginners who just want quick one-step automation, or anyone needing a very simple, friction-free "when X happens do Y" — that's Zapier or IFTTT territory, and Make's extra power is overhead you'd pay for in complexity.

## Verdict & rating

**Rating: 4.4/5.** Make earns this because it genuinely does more than its competitors for complex, branching automation, and it does it visually. The dents are the real learning curve and the operations limits at the cheap tiers, but for anyone automating non-trivial business flows, the power justifies the climb.

**Bottom line:** if your automations have grown past "trigger + action" and you need branching, loops, and error handling without writing code, Make is a top choice. Start on the free tier, build one real scenario, and you'll know within a few sessions whether the visual model is for you.

## FAQ

### Q: Is Make better than Zapier?
For complex, branching automations, yes — Make's visual canvas and routers let you build logic that Zapier's linear trigger-action model handles awkwardly. For simple one-step tasks with zero learning curve, Zapier is faster to set up. It comes down to complexity: yes if you need branches, no if you just want quick syncs.

### Q: How much does Make cost?
Make has a free tier (1,000 operations/month) and paid plans starting around $9–10/month on the Core tier. Prices are current as of October 2026 and operations scale with higher tiers.

### Q: Does Make require coding?
No. It's designed for non-programmers — you drag modules onto a canvas and wire them together visually. That said, the branching and data-mapping model has a technical feel, so a comfort with logic helps more than a comfort with syntax.

### Q: Can Make automate AI workflows?
Yes. Make has integrations with AI models and services (including calling LLM APIs via webhooks and built-in modules), so you can insert AI into automations — e.g. classify text, summarize, or generate content mid-flow.

### Q: What is a scenario in Make?
A scenario is a Make automation: a visual map of modules and connections that runs on a schedule, a webhook, or app triggers. Scenarios can branch with routers, loop over data, and handle errors.
