# Wave — Full Review & Verdict

Wave is the outlier in accounting software: its core bookkeeping, invoicing, and receipt tracking are genuinely free, with no trial countdown and no hidden paywall on the basics. For a micro-business, freelancer, or solopreneur whose accounting needs are real but not complicated, it's about the best $0 you can spend on your books. The catch is that the free engine charges you elsewhere — payment processing and payroll come with fees.

**Verdict: 4.0/5.** Best for micro-businesses and solopreneurs who want a real double-entry accounting tool without paying a subscription. Not for growing companies that need advanced inventory, project costing across fixed budgets, or free payroll — those needs point toward paid add-ons or a different platform.

## What it is

Wave (Wave Financial) is a cloud accounting platform aimed squarely at very small businesses, freelancers, and the self-employed. It competes in the same space as QuickBooks Self-Employed and Xero, but its core differentiator is the business model: the accounting, invoicing, receipt-scanning, and reporting features are free, and Wave makes money on per-transaction payment-processing fees and on paid payroll.

That trade gets criticised sometimes — the features you'd expect to pay for are free, while the ones tied to handling money carry transaction fees. But for someone who wants clean, real books without a monthly subscription, the free core is a legitimate engine rather than a teaser.

Where QuickBooks locks most of its value behind paid self-employment plans and Xero charges from the first seat, Wave just gives the core away. That changes the calculus for the smallest operators: the barrier to having actual accounting isn't a subscription, it's only the effort of setting it up. For a micro-business that has been running on a spreadsheet and a pile of receipts, that's a meaningful threshold removed.

## How it works / key features

### Free double-entry accounting

Wave covers income and expense tracking, bank account reconciliation, and the reports you actually need as a small business — profit and loss, balance sheet, cash flow, and a handful of others. For a solo operator, this replaces a spreadsheet and a shoebox of receipts with something that produces real accounting output.

The reports matter even if you never open them yourself: when you need a profit-and-loss figure for a loan application, a grant, or a tax appointment, it's a click away rather than a reconstruction job. Because the data is entered through invoices and categorised bank transactions, it stays current enough to be useful.

### Invoicing with tracking

You can build branded invoices, set them up to recur, and auto-charge saved payment methods. Invoices show you what's sent, viewed, and paid, so follow-up is easy. This is the tool most solo operators will use day to day, and it's genuinely free with no per-invoice charge.

The visibility is the quiet win. Knowing whether a client has opened an invoice, and which invoices are overdue, turns follow-up from an awkward guessing game into a simple, data-driven list. Combined with recurrence for monthly retainers, it reduces the repetitive admin that eats freelancer time.

### Receipt scanning and expense tracking

The connected mobile app reads and categorises receipts, so expense tracking is less manual. This feeds directly into the reports, keeping the books current without data entry chores.

The practical benefit is at tax time: instead of sorting a year of paper receipts, your expenses are already categorised and summed. For a self-employed person who has ever stared at a bag of receipts in April, this alone can justify adopting Wave.

### Payments

Wave Payments lets clients pay invoices by card or bank transfer (ACH). This is where the free model ends: it charges a per-transaction fee on card payments and a lower rate on ACH. Easy for the client, and the money lands in your account via no monthly platform cost, but the processing fee is the real cost of the "free" invoice.

The honest reading of Payments is that it's a convenience for you and your clients, priced per transaction rather than per month. If most of your clients pay by bank transfer or check, you can lean on the free invoicing and barely touch Payments at all. If clients prefer cards, budget for the processing fee or factor it into pricing.

### Payroll (paid add-on)

For US customers, Wave Payroll handles salaried and contractor payments with tax filing, on a per-employee fee. For a micro-business with a couple of staff this can be a fair deal, but it's a paid product on top of the free core and only serves US-based businesses.

For the overwhelming majority of micro-businesses on bank-transfer invoicing, however, Wave is close to a permanent $0 cost.

## Where it shines / where it falls short

**Strengths**
- **The core is truly free.** Real double-entry accounting, invoicing, and reports with no trial timer and no paywall on the fundamentals is rare, and Wave does it honestly.
- **Easy to start.** Onboarding and the daily flow are approachable for someone who isn't an accountant; you can go from signup to sending an invoice in minutes.
- **Reasonable reporting.** The standard small-business reports are there and usable, which is more than most "free" finance tools offer.
- **Receipt scanning with the mobile app.** Less manual data entry keeps the books honest.

**Weaknesses**
- **Paid add-ons are where the money is.** Payment processing fees and payroll cost real money on top of the "free" headline.
- **No advanced inventory or job-costing depth.** If your business manages stock or needs project profitability across complex work, Wave gets thin fast.
- **Payroll is US-only.** Non-US micro-businesses can't use it and must find payroll elsewhere.

## Best for / not for

Best for **micro-businesses, freelancers, and solopreneurs** — landscapers, tutors, designers, consultants, sole traders — who need legitimate double-entry accounting, invoicing, and reports without paying a monthly subscription, and who don't rely heavily on card processing.

Not ideal for **inventory-heavy retail or e-commerce** operations that need stock management baked in, **companies that need powerful job/project costing** across a large team, or **anyone who needs free payroll** — that's a paid, US-only add-on.

## How to decide

The honest test is volume and payment method. If you issue invoices and most money arrives by bank transfer, Wave costs you nothing and covers the essentials — start there and you've likely found your long-term accounting home. If your clients overwhelmingly pay by card, total the annual processing fees before committing; at high volume a flat-fee processor plus free billing might be cheaper overall.

And if your business already carries inventory or runs multi-step project costing, skip straight past Wave — those are the capabilities its free model deliberately leaves thin, and forcing them in will frustrate you more than a modest subscription to a fuller tool.

## Verdict & rating

**Rating: 4.0/5.** Wave earns this because it delivers genuinely free, credible accounting to the smallest businesses, and the lack of a subscription is a real and unusual advantage. It's docked for the honest revenue model — payment and payroll fees are where the money comes out — and for lacking depth in inventory and complex costing.

**Bottom line:** if you're a micro-business or freelancer who wants real books without a monthly bill, start with Wave — it's the strongest free option at this size. Upgrade, add payment processing, or move on only when your business grows past what the free engine comfortably covers.

## FAQ

### Q: Is Wave really free?
Yes, for the core product. Accounting, invoicing, receipt tracking, and the standard reports have no subscription fee. Wave makes money on per-transaction payment-processing fees and on its paid (US) payroll product.

### Q: How does Wave make money if it's free?
Through fees on payment processing and through Wave Payroll. So the accounting engine is free, but handling payments or running payroll is where costs enter.

### Q: Is Wave good for invoicing?
Yes — invoicing is one of its strongest free features, with branded templates, recurring invoices, and auto-charge options, and it's free with no per-invoice fee.

### Q: Does Wave handle payroll?
For US customers, yes, as a paid add-on per employee. Outside the US, Wave payroll isn't available.

### Q: Is Wave better than QuickBooks?
For the smallest businesses and freelancers, Wave is usually the better value because the core is free and QuickBooks charges a subscription for comparable features. As your needs grow past simple accounting — inventory, deeper costing — QuickBooks' paid tiers offer more.

### Q: Can I accept credit card payments with Wave?
Yes, through Wave Payments, which lets clients pay invoices online by card or bank transfer. Card payments carry a per-transaction processing fee; bank transfer is cheaper.
