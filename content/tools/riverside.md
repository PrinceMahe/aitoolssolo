# Riverside — Full Review & Verdict

Riverside is a remote recording platform built for audio/video quality, and it solves the classic podcast problem: guests on bad internet don't ruin the recording. **Verdict: 4.5/5.** Best for podcasters and interviewers who want studio-quality, separate-track recordings every time, with AI transcripts and clips thrown in. It has a free tier and a Pro plan around $15/month.

## What it is

Riverside is a browser-based remote recording platform (with a mobile app) designed for podcasts, interviews, and video content. Founded by the same team behind Jotform, it's positioned specifically as a tool where **recording quality is the product** — aimed at people for whom a clean, reliable, multi-track recording matters more than just "having a video call."

Its core design decision is the differentiator: instead of streaming a single compressed file (which breaks when internet drops), each participant records **locally, in high quality, on their own device**, and those local files are uploaded and synced afterward. That means even if a call drops or a guest's Wi-Fi is shaky, the audio/video captured on each side is still studio-grade. This local-recording approach is what separates Riverside from a standard call tool like Zoom or Meet.

## How it works / key features

**4K local recording with separate tracks.** Every participant records a local, uncompressed-quality file, so you get isolated audio and video tracks per person rather than one mixed stream. This is huge for editing — you can fix one person's audio, cut without affecting others, and produce true stereo from separate channels.

**Local, device-side recording = reliability.** Because each side records locally and syncs after the call, a flaky connection doesn't degrade the final audio. The recording resilience is the reason interviewers trust it for high-stakes guest recordings.

**Magic Audio.** Riverside's AI audio-processing feature cleans up background noise, echo, and leveled audio automatically, so you get more polished sound without manual EQ. Useful for episodes where guests record from less-than-ideal rooms.

**AI transcripts and clips.** Riverside automatically transcribes each recording and generates highlight clips — short, shareable moments cut for social or trailers. This turns raw recordings into publishable assets (show notes, captions, promo clips) with less manual editing.

**Text-based editing.** You can edit the audio by editing the transcript — cut words or sentences in the text and the corresponding audio is removed. That's a genuinely lower-friction way to tighten an episode than scrubbing a waveform.

## Where it shines / where it falls short

**Strengths**
- **Studio-quality, reliable recordings.** Local recording means guests' bad internet doesn't ruin the take — the #1 reason to pick it over a normal call tool.
- **Separate audio/video tracks per participant.** Massively easier, better editing than a single mixed file.
- **Genuinely useful AI that saves time.** Transcriptions, highlight clips, and Magic Audio cut the production workload.
- **Simple for guests.** No app install needed — a browser link works, which lowers the barrier for less-technical interviewees.

**Weaknesses**
- **Full features need Pro.** The free tier is limited, so regular use effectively means paying ~$15/month.
- **Some advanced export/feature options sit in paid tiers** — if you need high-end export flexibility you'll be on a paid plan.
- **Browser/app-dependent on schedule.** While local recording is robust once rolling, the platform still relies on the web app experience, so setup friction exists for the very first connect if a guest is unfamiliar.

## Best for / not for

Best for **podcasters, interviewers, and video content creators** who value clean, reliable, multi-track recordings over convenience, and who want the AI assistance (transcripts, clips, cleanup) to cut post-production. It's also strong for remote **team shows** and **client-facing interviews** where the recorded quality reflects on you.

Not ideal for **casual video callers** who just need a quick conversation — Zoom or Meet is simpler and cheaper for that. If you never edit or care about multi-track audio, you're paying for capabilities you won't use.

## Verdict & rating

**Rating: 4.5/5.** Riverside earns it because it solves the hardest problem in remote recording — delivering studio-grade, separate-track audio reliably regardless of guest connection — and wraps it in genuinely useful AI tools that cut editing time. It loses points only on the free tier being limited and the need to pay for full capabilities.

**Bottom line:** if you're serious about podcast or interview recording, Riverside is one of the best tools available. Start free to confirm the workflow, then move to Pro if you produce regularly.

## FAQ

### Q: How much does Riverside cost?
Riverside has a free tier and a paid Pro plan around $15/month (with annual billing). The free tier is nice for testing but limited; regular production pretty much requires the paid plan. Prices are current as of October 2026.

### Q: Why is Riverside better than recording in Zoom?
Riverside records each participant locally in high quality on their own device, then syncs the files — so a guest's bad internet drops or freezes don't degrade the audio quality, and you get separate tracks per person instead of one compressed mix. That's the key reason serious podcasters prefer it.

### Q: Does Riverside record video?
Yes, it supports both audio-only and video recording (up to 4K per participant, locally recorded) with separate tracks, which makes it a choice for video podcasts and interviews, not just audio.

### Q: What AI features does Riverside include?
It includes automatic transcription, highlight-clip generation shareable to social, Magic Audio (noise/echo cleanup), and text-based editing where cutting words in the transcript edits the audio.
